About The Unsaid Thought

Some thoughts are said and bought out into words, some of these words find their place in pages and the deep hidden meaning stays in heart and minds for long. This is what The Unsaid Thought brings us about, the words, the thoughts, the memories and all of the above hidden meanings of emotions we experience.

About the website

The Unsaid Thought is a space for poetry,emotions, whatever we reflect from our experience and all of our thoughts tthat often remain unspoken. It is a place where silence finds words in poems woven in deep meanings and some of the ordinary moments become the stories remaining to find their extraordinary meanings.

About the writer

I’m Nidhi, a poet, writer, technologist, and an explorer of thoughts that often remain unspoken. Born on December 12, 2002, in India, I have always found myself drawn to two seemingly different worlds—the world of technology and the world of emotions. I graduated in Electronics and Communication Engineering, and along the way, developed a strong interest in web development, artificial intelligence, and Generative AI. Technology taught me how to build things, solve problems, and turn ideas into reality. But poetry taught me something different: how to listen to the thoughts that cannot always be explained.Writing has always been a part of me. I write about emotions, silence, relationships, memories, dreams, loneliness, hope, and the countless thoughts that pass through the human mind but often remain unsaid. For me, poetry is not merely about words or rhymes—it is about giving a voice to feelings that struggle to find one. The Unsaid Thought is a small reflection of that part of me. It is a space where thoughts become words, emotions become verses, and the things left unsaid finally get a place to exist. Alongside writing, I continue to explore technology, web development, AI, and Generative AI. I enjoy creating, learning, experimenting, and discovering how technology can shape the way we express ourselves.

Words that are unsaid,
finally finding their place.
-Nidhi Chandra
